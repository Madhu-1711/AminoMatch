TRANSPARENT BY DESIGN

A closer look at the numbers.

Understand the comparison before drawing conclusions.

What does the score mean?

Your body needs nine essential amino acids from food: histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine. Protein grams alone do not show their proportions.

We compare those proportions with an adult reference pattern. The pattern score is the lowest of the nine group percentages. All nine must reach 100% for the label “Meets the reference pattern.” Percentages above 100% are shown, while the bars stop at 100%.

Why are two groups combined?

The WHO/FAO/UNU 2007 adult scoring pattern groups methionine + cysteine and phenylalanine + tyrosine. Cysteine and tyrosine are supporting amino acids, not part of the nine-essential-amino-acid total. They are included only in the group comparison.

What it does not tell you

  • This is not a daily requirement tracker. A small portion can meet the pattern without supplying enough total food or protein.
  • This is not DIAAS or PDCAAS: digestion, absorption and amino acid availability are not modeled.
  • Complementary foods do not necessarily need to be eaten in the same meal.
  • Starter values are illustrative, not verified measurements. Uploaded values are not independently verified either. Food preparation and source quality matter.
  • Pairing ideas compare equal 100 g portions and are not personalized dietary recommendations.
